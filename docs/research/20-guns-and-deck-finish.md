# Guns and deck finish, October 2026

Keep the established Surprise armament and dimensions in `spec.js`: 24 nine-pounders, ten four-pounders and six carronades. This pass corrects the rendered mountings and physical openings; it does not substitute a different ship's guns.

Construction references:

- [Queen Anne's Revenge conservation: gun carriage](https://www.qaronline.org/conservation/artifacts/arms-and-armament/gun-carriage): solid wooden trucks, axletrees, cheeks, quoin, cap-squares and rope fittings. An earlier-period construction comparator, not a Surprise source.
- [Royal Museums Greenwich: Common ship or Garrison Carriage, ZAZ6985](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-86776): views of a carriage and its axletrees. This is a 1966 drawing of a circa-1840 carriage, not an 1805 plan.
- [Royal Museums Greenwich: sectional ordnance model](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-68892): elevating screw and tackle eyes. A nineteenth-century comparator.
- [USS Constitution Museum: Daily Routine at Sea](https://ussconstitutionmuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2019/05/Daily-Routine-at-Sea.pdf): holystoning and washing the deck, supporting restrained scrubbed timber rather than heavily rotten decking. American naval practice is a comparator, not evidence of Surprise's exact plank layout.

The exact upper port placement, wood grain, block shape and weathering remain a reconstruction. Upper openings follow the existing gun stations and bore heights. Openings cut both exterior and interior faces and have timber reveals; barrels no longer pass through an opaque bulwark. Fine tackle and ironwork remain distance-controlled. Cast iron has a subdued rough finish and deep bores; carriage timber has physical-scale grain. The release checks clear ports, preserved side planking, deck contact, forty barrels, finite geometry and browser-tier budgets.
