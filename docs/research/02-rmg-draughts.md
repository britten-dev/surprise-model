# 02 — RMG / NMM draughts and models of UNITE / SURPRISE

Research for 3D modelling. Every number carries an inline source. 1 ft = 0.3048 m.

## 0. HEADLINE WARNING — TWO DIFFERENT SHIPS, AND THE RMG CATALOGUE CONFLATES THEM

There were **two** captured French ships in British hands in 1796 whose French name was
*Unité*. The RMG online catalogue links the plans of the **wrong one** to the vessel record
"Surprise (captured 1796)". Do not order or trace ZAZ3181–ZAZ3184.

| | **Ship A — our SURPRISE** | **Ship B — HMS UNITE** |
|---|---|---|
| French origin | corvette *L'Unité*, launched 16 Jan 1794, Le Havre, built Jean Fouache, designed P-A Forfait, *Unité* class 1793 | frigate launched 18 May 1787 as *Gracieuse*, renamed *Unité* 28 Sep 1793; *Charmante* class 1777, Rochefort |
| Captured | 20 Apr 1796 by HMS Inconstant | 11–12 Apr 1796 off Île d'Yeu |
| RN name | **SURPRISE** (28 nominal guns, Sixth Rate) | **UNITE** (32 nominal guns, Fifth Rate), named 25 Apr 1796 |
| Length of gundeck | **126 ft 0 in / 38.405 m** | 142 ft 5½ in / 43.410 m |
| Breadth | **31 ft 8 in / 9.652 m** | 37 ft 8 in / 11.481 m |
| Burthen | **578 73/94 tons bm** | 873 71/94 tons bm |
| Fitted at Plymouth | Jan–May **1798** | 25 Apr – 4 Dec **1796** |
| RMG plans | **ZAZ3067, ZAZ3068** | ZAZ3181, ZAZ3182, ZAZ3183, ZAZ3184 |

Sources: [threedecks Surprise (1796) id 6983](https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=6983) ·
[threedecks Unite (1796) id 7261](https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=7261) ·
[Wikipedia, Charmante-class frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charmante-class_frigate) ·
[Wikipedia, French frigate Gracieuse (1787)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_frigate_Gracieuse_(1787)) ·
both threedecks entries cite Rif Winfield, *British Warships in the Age of Sail 1793–1817* ("BWAS-1793").

**Proof the ZAZ3181–3184 plans are Ship B, not Surprise:**

1. Their own catalogue text says "fitted at Plymouth Dockyard for a **32-gun, Fifth Rate**
   Frigate" — Surprise was a 28-gun Sixth Rate.
   ([ZAZ3181](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-82972))
2. They are dated **June / July / October 1796**. Surprise sailed for Jamaica 29 Jul 1796 and
   did not enter Plymouth Dockyard until 21 Jan 1798.
   ([threedecks 6983](https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=6983))
   HMS Unite *was* at Plymouth for exactly those months.
3. **Read off the plan itself.** The title cartouche of ZAZ3181 says only **"L'UNITE"**.
   The title cartouche of ZAZ3067 says **"SURPRISE late L'UNITE"**. Verified by inspecting
   the museum's own scans, [j6091.jpg](https://collections.rmg.co.uk/media/2/441/644/j6091.jpg)
   and [j5948.jpg](https://collections.rmg.co.uk/media/2/440/707/j5948.jpg).
4. The two hulls drawn are visibly different: ZAZ3181 shows a much fuller midship section,
   an elaborate beakhead and figurehead; ZAZ3067 shows a finer corvette hull.

**Also do not confuse with:** HMS *Surprize* (1774), an Enterprize-class 28-gun Sixth Rate.
Her plans are RMG ZAZ (objects 83077, 83174, 83189) and print J6481. Different ship entirely.
([RMG object 83189](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-83189))

### WHAT DOES NOT EXIST AT RMG (explicit gaps)

Stated so the modeller stops looking:
- **No "as taken off" survey draught of Surprise.** The only "as taken off" draught in the
  catalogue is ZAZ3181, and that is the *other* ship. If a 1796 Portsmouth/Plymouth survey
  draught of the corvette was ever made, it is not in Collections Online.
- **No upper (gun) deck plan** of Surprise.
- **No lower deck / platform plan** of Surprise.
- **No separate inboard-profile plan** — the inboard detail exists only as the red overlay on
  ZAZ3067.
- **No framing / disposition-of-frames plan**, so frame spacing must be taken off the
  half-breadth station lines on ZAZ3067 or reconstructed.
- **No contemporary ship model.** Searches of RMG Collections Online for "surprise 1796",
  "unite 1796", "unite corvette model" and "variante" return no SLR model record for either
  vessel. There is a painting, *The cutting out of HMS Hermione, 24 October 1799*
  (rmgc-object-12011), and a Naval General Service Medal (rmgc-object-41238), but no model.
  Search used: https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/search/surprise%201796

### Internal arithmetic check — these dimensions are self-consistent

Builder's Old Measurement: tons = (keel for tonnage x breadth x breadth/2) / 94.

(108.5104 x 31.6667 x 15.83335) / 94 = **578.78 = 578 73/94** ✅ exact.

So keel-for-tonnage 108 ft 6⅛ in and breadth 31 ft 8 in are confirmed as the surveyed pair.
(Rule and formula: standard BOM, see e.g. [Wikipedia, Builder's Old Measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Builder%27s_Old_Measurement).)
Had the keel been 108 ft 6¼ in the result would be 578.84, which does not reduce to /94 — a
further small argument that 6⅛ in is the correct fraction.

### RECONSTRUCTED — breadth moulded

**Not found.** The row exists on ZAZ3067 but the figure cannot be resolved in the free 1280 px
scan, and no published source quotes it.

- **RECONSTRUCTED value: 31 ft 2 in = 9.500 m.**
- Rule used: moulded breadth = extreme breadth minus twice the thickness of the bottom/side
  plank at the main breadth. For a vessel of roughly 550–600 tons, Steel's tables give bottom
  plank of about 3 in, so 31 ft 8 in − 2 x 3 in = 31 ft 2 in.
  Rule source: David Steel, *The Elements and Practice of Naval Architecture* (1805), scantling
  tables — scan at https://archive.org/details/elementspractice00stee
- Independent support: measuring the widest moulded line on the ZAZ3067 body plan gives
  **30.9 ft (9.42 m)** at 1:48 (see §3), i.e. within 1% of 31 ft 2 in and 2.5% below the
  extreme breadth. **Mark as RECONSTRUCTED, not fact.**

## 3. SCALE AND HOW TO WORK OFF THE FREE SCANS — measured, not assumed

Neither Surprise record states a scale. Both were measured from the museum's own JPEGs.

**ZAZ3067 (j5948.jpg, 1280 x 451 px, sheet 1350 x 470 mm)**

- Sheet-to-pixel: 1350 / 1280 = **1.0547 mm of sheet per pixel**.
- Widest moulded line of the body plan measured **186 px = 196.2 mm on the sheet**.
- At 1:48 that is 9.42 m = **30.89 ft** — against a recorded extreme breadth of 31.67 ft and a
  reconstructed moulded breadth of 31.17 ft. Error against moulded: **0.9%**.
- At 1:64 it would give 41.2 ft, at 1:96 it would give 61.8 ft — both absurd.
- **Conclusion: ZAZ3067 is drawn at 1:48 (¼ inch to 1 foot), the standard Admiralty draught
  scale.** Consistent with ZAZ3181, the companion Plymouth/Marshall draught, whose catalogue
  record *does* state 1:48 and whose sheet is 463 x 1342 mm — near-identical.

Working factors for the 1280 px JPEG of ZAZ3067:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Sheet mm per pixel | 1.0547 |
| **Real-ship mm per pixel** | **50.63** |
| **Real-ship inches per pixel** | **1.993** (call it 2 in) |
| **Pixels per foot of ship** | **6.021** |
| Sheet represents (at 1:48) | 64.80 m x 22.56 m = 212.6 ft x 74.0 ft |
| Base line (underside of keel) in the JPEG | y = 273 px; the scale-bar box top edge is y = 282 px |
| Printed scale bar in the JPEG | x = 306 to x = 1104 px, i.e. 798 px = 841.6 mm of sheet ≈ 132 ft of ship at 1:48 |

Aspect-ratio caveat: 1350/470 = 2.872 but 1280/451 = 2.838, a **1.2% mismatch**, so the JPEG
either includes a sliver of mount or the catalogued sheet size is rounded. Treat every derived
figure above as **±1–2%**. For real work, buy the print or license the high-resolution master.

**ZAZ3068 (j5947.jpg, 1280 x 389 px, sheet 935 x 280 mm)**

- Sheet-to-pixel: 935 / 1280 = **0.7305 mm per pixel**.
- Deck outline measured 1169 px long = 853.9 mm of sheet → at 1:48 = **134.5 ft**, correct for
  a quarterdeck-plus-forecastle plan of a 126 ft ship once the counter aft and the catheads
  forward are included. Widest point measured 287 px = 209.7 mm → **33.0 ft** at 1:48, again
  correct once the catheads and channels are in.
- At 1:64 those become 179 ft and 44 ft; at 1:96, 269 ft and 66 ft. Both impossible.
- **Conclusion: ZAZ3068 is also 1:48.**

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Sheet mm per pixel | 0.7305 |
| **Real-ship mm per pixel** | **35.06** |
| **Real-ship inches per pixel** | **1.380** |
| **Pixels per foot of ship** | **8.692** |
| Aspect caveat | 935/280 = 3.339 vs 1280/389 = 3.290, **1.5% mismatch**; treat as ±1–2% |

Higher-resolution options: prints.rmg.co.uk sells both at full size, no border, £45.00
(J5948, J5947). Digital masters are licensed through RMG Images
(https://images.rmg.co.uk/, asset 16480 for J5948) — that site is behind Cloudflare and could
not be read here.

Verbatim ZAZ3181 description: "Scale 1:48. Plan showing the body plan with sternboard
decoration and name in a cartouche on stern counter, sheer lines with inboard detail and
figurehead, and longitudinal half-breadth for Unite (1796), a captured French Frigate, as taken
off at Plymouth Dockyard prior to fitting as a 32-gun, Fifth Rate Frigate. Signed J. Marshall.
(Master Shipwright)"

**Second catalogue error, ZAZ3183.** The record says 1:48 on a 450 x 532 mm sheet. At 1:48 the
532 mm dimension represents only 25.5 m = 83.7 ft — too short for either candidate ship's deck
(126 ft or 142 ft 5½ in). At 1:96 the 142 ft 5½ in gundeck of HMS Unite scales to 451 mm, which
matches the 450 mm sheet dimension almost exactly. **ZAZ3183 is almost certainly 1:96, like its
companion ZAZ3184.** Flagged, not proven.

**Sheet-size sanity note.** ZAZ3181's 1342 mm sheet at a confirmed 1:48 gives 64.4 m = 211 ft of
drawn width — comfortable for a 142 ft ship with a body plan alongside, and generous for a 126 ft
ship. This is a weak argument on its own, which is why the title-cartouche reading in §0 is the
one to rely on.

## 5. PROGRESS BOOKS AND SHIP'S BOOK — where the survey data lives

- **NMM Progress Book, volume 5, folio 268** is the Surprise entry, quoted verbatim in both
  Surprise plan records:
  *"'Surprize' arrived at Plymouth Dockyard on 21 January 1798, was docked on 15 February to be
  recoppered, and launched on 2 March. She sailed having been fitted on 2 May 1798 having cost
  £6,992."*
  Source: https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-82858
- The Progress Books at the NMM are held as **PST/49** — "bound photocopies of documents held in
  the National Archives (catalogue reference ADM/180)". They "list building and repairs of all
  classes of naval vessels usually with time, place and cost of building, docking and
  undocking, graving and cleaning, fitting and refitting. Some survey information is also
  included." An index to ships' names exists.
  Source: [RMG Research guide B6: The Royal Navy: Administrative records](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/research-guides/research-guide-b6-royal-navy-administrative-records)
- Same guide's caveat, important here: the Progress Books are fullest c.1730–1780, get less
  precise after the American War, and **by 1800 entries are often consolidated or omitted**. So
  do not expect rich Surprise data beyond the folio 268 entry.
- TNA series: **ADM 180, "Navy Board and Admiralty: Progress and Dimension Books"**, covering
  date, cost and nature of building, repair and refitting work on HM ships 1620–1912.
  https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C1889
- **There is no "ship's book" for Surprise.** TNA **ADM 135** (Ships' Books) covers **1807–1873**
  — Surprise was sold in February 1802, five years before the series begins.
  (TNA series description, via https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/royal-navy-operations-correspondence-1660-1914/)
  The **Dimension Books within ADM 180** are the nearest thing to a survey record and are the
  next place to look for the moulded breadth and the "as taken off" figures. **Not consulted —
  requires a visit to Kew or the Caird Library.**

## 7. OPEN ITEMS

1. **Buy or license J5948 at full resolution** and read the moulded breadth and the Admiralty
   register number off the title cartouche. Both are on the sheet; the free 1280 px scan is too
   coarse.
2. **ADM 180 Dimension Books at TNA** — the likely home of the full 1796 survey "as taken off"
   at capture, if one was taken.
3. **Confirm the ZAZ3183 scale** (1:48 as catalogued vs 1:96 as argued in §4) if that plan is
   ever needed — though it is the wrong ship anyway.
4. **Report the ZAZ3181–3184 mis-attribution to RMG.** Their vessel-authority record links four
   HMS Unite plans to Surprise.
5. **No contemporary model exists**, so no independent check on the decorative work, the head,
   or the stern. Those must come off ZAZ3067's body plan / stern elevation and from French
   corvette practice.
6. threedecks.org blocks automated fetching (HTTP 403 to standard tooling); the data above was
   retrieved with a browser user-agent. Cite the page, expect to open it by hand.
