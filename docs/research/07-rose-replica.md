# 07 — The San Diego replica ("HMS Surprise", ex-"HMS Rose", 1970)

**Purpose of this file:** (a) a sanity cross-check on plausible spar and hull numbers;
(b) an explicit list of what **NOT** to copy. Our model is the French-built *Unité* of
1794 / HMS *Surprise* of 1796. The San Diego ship is a **1757 British hull with a
Hollywood face**. Almost nothing on her is evidence for our model.

Conversion used throughout: 1 ft = 0.3048 m.

**Conflict — trust the 130 ft.** 130 ft above deck for the main truck is consistent with
a 135 ft hull and with every photograph; 170 ft would give a mast-height/LOD ratio of
1.26, far outside any square-rigger norm. The 170 ft figure is boatyard marketing copy
and may be measured from the keel, or may simply be loose. **Use 130 ft (39.6 m).**

**Individual spar dimensions (mast, topmast, topgallant, yard lengths and diameters) —
NOT FOUND.** No published spar table for this ship exists online: the Bolger rig plan is
in *30-Odd Boats* (International Marine, 1982, ISBN 0877421528, cited by Wikipedia) and
is not digitised. Do not invent one from her; use the period sources for our ship instead.

RECONSTRUCTED cross-check only (do **not** use as the replica's real numbers): if her
main truck is 130 ft (39.62 m) above deck and her deck length is 135 ft 6 in (41.30 m),
her main-truck-height / LOD ratio is **0.96**. For the historical *Surprise*
(gundeck 126 ft 0 in / 38.40 m) the same ratio would put the main truck at about
**121 ft (36.9 m)** above deck. Rule used: geometric similarity of the two rigs, which
is only defensible because both are ship-rigged vessels of near-identical breadth. Treat
as a plausibility band, not a number.

RECONSTRUCTED gunport spacing, replica: 13 ports spread over roughly 118 ft (36 m) of

of size, not of design.

### 3.1 The head-to-head numbers

¹ https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Rose_(1757)
² https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=6983 (source cited there:

**Tonnage vs displacement:** 578 ⁷³⁄₉₄ tons bm is a *capacity* figure from the Builder's
Old Measurement rule, not a weight. Do not set it against the replica's 500 long tons of
displacement. Wikipedia's "657 tons" for *Surprise* is the displacement-equivalent figure.

**A source conflict worth recording.** Kolthoff's SNAME comparison table (slide 9 of
https://www.thedearsurprise.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/2007-10_Reviving_Surprise.pdf)
gives *Surprise* (1796) as **beam 34 ft 8 in** and **displacement 378 tons**, and *Rose*
(1756) as beam 30 ft 4 in / 430 tons. **Do not use Kolthoff's beam or tonnage.** His
34 ft 8 in for *Surprise* is 3 ft wider than the Admiralty survey figure carried by
Winfield, threedecks and Wikipedia (31 ft 8 in) and looks like a transcription slip
(3 for 1); his 378 tons looks like the same slip on 578. His *lengths*, however, agree
with the good sources and are usable. Trust **31 ft 8 in** and **578 ⁷³⁄₉₄ tons bm**.

This is the list of things the replica gets wrong for our purposes. Primary evidence is
Kolthoff's side-by-side of the two Admiralty draughts, slides 6 and 8 of

RECONSTRUCTED, for #17 only: British practice of the 1790s took in the topside by
roughly one twelfth to one fifteenth of the extreme breadth per side between the
main wale and the gunwale; French practice of the same date was generally *more*
pronounced. On a 31 ft 8 in beam that is of the order of **1 ft 3 in – 1 ft 6 in
(0.38–0.46 m) per side**. This is a rule-of-thumb band, stated as such, and is a
placeholder until the *Unité* draught or Marquardt gives a measured figure. **Not fact.**

## 4. The modifications made for the film (2001)

Primary source for this section is David L. Kolthoff, P.E., *Reviving Surprise:
Reconfiguration of "HMS" Rose into "HMS" Surprise for Her Role in "Master & Commander",
and Restoring her Certification*, Society of Naval Architects and Marine Engineers,
San Diego Section, 16 October 2007 —
https://www.thedearsurprise.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/2007-10_Reviving_Surprise.pdf
(slides 18–20 and 31). Corroborated by the Wikipedia summary, which cites Eric Quinlan,
"Cross Currents", *Yachting*, Nov 2003.

### 4.1 Hull

- **Bow rebuilt from flat to round** — the square front of the 1757 forecastle head cut
  away and reframed as a round bow, to match the 1796 ship's round forecastle front.
  (Kolthoff slide 18; the change is documented photographically on slides 21–22.)
- **Transom changed** — the stern completely reworked (Kolthoff slide 18, photographs
  slide 23). Wikipedia: "a reshaped stern". The result is the tall round-headed
  ornamented transom with a full row of glazed stern lights and deep glazed quarter
  galleries seen today; compare her 2000 stern, which was a plain low counter with a
  single window row, an oval medallion and blind quarter badges
  (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:HMS_Rose_-_2000_stern_51735471_6ed0c47787.jpg).
- **All deckhouses and coamings removed**, including the sail-training deckhouses
  (Kolthoff slides 18, 24–25). Wikipedia: "all deck structures removed".
- **Bulwarks raised** and **hammock cranes/frames added** along the rail
  (Kolthoff slide 18, photographs slide 26).
- **Billethead changed; figurehead replaced** — the crew's "Chester" replaced by
  "Athena" (Kolthoff slides 18 and 27). Note: the museum's own display and several
  popular accounts call the present figurehead **Boadicea**; both names circulate. Record
  the disagreement; do not treat either as settled.
- **Copper sheeting added to the hull** (Kolthoff slide 18, photographs slide 28). Removed
  or painted over at the 2007 drydocking: "Hull painted. (So long, copper sheathing.)"
  (Kolthoff slide 37).
- Kolthoff, slide 20: at Captain Richard Bailey's insistence "all ship changes were
  permanent and to marine standards. **No temporary sets.**" That is important — the film
  changes are structural, so a modeller cannot treat them as removable dressing.

### 4.2 Steering and mechanicals

- Single ship's wheel at the transom **replaced by a double wheel**, plus a below-deck
  control station and **hydraulic steering** (Kolthoff slides 18, 24–25, 29; Wikipedia).

### 4.3 Rig

- **Royal masts and yards added to fore and main.**
- **Studding sails (and their booms/irons) added.**
- **Mizzen** changed from a single topsail to **topsail and topgallant**.
- **Wire standing rigging "bulked up"** so that it reads on camera as hemp.
- **New sails** throughout (Wikipedia; Kolthoff slides 19 and 30–31).
- **Period fighting tops fitted** (Wikipedia, citing Quinlan).
- For the gimbal-mounted work at Baja Studios the production also built **shortened fore
  and main masts**, with the upper masts, rigging and sails extended digitally — that is a
  separate stage rig, not part of the ship (https://theasc.com/article/hell-high-water-master-commander/
  as summarised at https://thedearsurprise.com/the-ships-sets-and-storms-in-master-and-commander-the-far-side-of-the-world/).

### 4.4 What the brief calls "false stern galleries" and "extended head"

- **Stern galleries:** confirmed in substance — the transom was rebuilt and glazed
  quarter galleries now exist where the ship previously carried blind quarter badges.
  The phrase "false" is fair in the sense that the galleries are cosmetic joinery on a
  1757 stern, but they are **permanent structure**, not scenery (Kolthoff slide 20).
- **Extended head:** **NOT CONFIRMED as an extension.** What is documented is a **change
  of bow form (flat to round)** and a **new billethead and figurehead**. No source reached
  here says the head was lengthened. Record it as "bow reformed and head refitted", not
  "head extended", until a source says otherwise.
- **Fibreglass gun barrels:** **NOT CONFIRMED** — see §2.3.

## 6. What NOT to copy — the short list

1. **Any hull dimension.** She is 7.5 % too long on deck, on effectively our beam. Her
   L/B is 4.23; ours is 3.98.
2. **Her sheer and deck breaks.** 1757 British Establishment profile: deep open waist,
   quarterdeck starting at the mainmast, high stepped quarterdeck. Our ship is a 1794
   French corvette.
3. **Her entry and run.** Bolger re-cut the underwater lines without authority; they are
   neither 1757 nor 1794.
4. **Her stern and quarter galleries.** Built in 2001 by a film production. There is no
   claim anywhere that they reproduce the *Surprise* draught.
5. **Her bow and head.** Reformed in 2001 from a square 1757 forecastle front; new
   billethead; figurehead named either Athena or Boadicea depending on the source —
   neither is documented on the real ship.
6. **Her rig proportions and spar plan.** Sized for a longer hull, with steel lower masts,
   wire standing rigging, and royals and studdingsails added for the camera in 2001.
7. **Her guns.** Prop pieces dressed as 24-pounders "for dramatic effect"; the real ship
   carried 24 × 9-pdr on the upper deck with 4-pdrs and 12-pdr carronades above.
8. **Her paint.** A plain ochre stripe with ochre port lids, black everywhere else,
   black bottom, no boot-top. It is a film art-department scheme.
9. **Her topside height / bulwark height / tumblehome.** Raised twice (1970 for headroom,
   2001 for the film).
10. **Her tonnage figures.** 500 long tons displacement is not comparable with
    578 ⁷³⁄₉₄ tons bm.

**What she is genuinely useful for:** a sanity band on scale. A wooden ship-rigged vessel
of ~126–136 ft on deck and ~32 ft beam displaces on the order of 500 long tons, draws
about 13 ft, and carries a main truck about 130 ft above deck with roughly 13,000 sq ft
of plain sail. If our reconstruction of the 1796 *Surprise* lands far outside those bands,
something is wrong.
