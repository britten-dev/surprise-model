# 01 — Marquardt plan set for HMS SURPRISE (ex-UNITÉ 1794)

Research for 3D modelling. Every number carries an inline source. Conversion used
throughout: 1 ft = 0.3048 m. Values I could not source are stated as NOT FOUND and, where
possible, followed by a RECONSTRUCTED figure with the rule and the rule's source.

## 0. CORRECTION TO THE BRIEF — READ FIRST

**There is no "Anatomy of the Ship: The Frigate Surprise" by Karl Heinz Marquardt.** The
brief's premise and both ISBNs are wrong. Verified:

| Claim in brief | Reality | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ISBN 0851779441 = Marquardt, *The Frigate Surprise*, Conway 2003 | = Brian King, *Workshop Practice for Ship Modellers*, Conway Maritime, 30 Oct 2003 | https://www.amazon.com/Workshop-Practice-Ship-Modellers-Occasional/dp/0851779441 |
| ISBN 1591140161 = Naval Institute Press edition | = Nancy Prothro Arbuthnot, *Guiding Lights: US Naval Academy Monuments and Memorials*, NIP 2012 | https://www.amazon.com/Guiding-Lights-Academy-Monuments-Memorials/dp/1591140161 |
| Marquardt AOTS volume on Surprise | Marquardt's AOTS volumes are **Endeavour (1995)**, **HMS Beagle (1999)**, **USS Constitution 44 (2005)**. No Surprise volume. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Heinz_Marquardt |

**The actual Marquardt Surprise plan set** is a commissioned chapter inside:

> Brian Lavery & Geoff Hunt, ***The Frigate Surprise: The Complete Story of the Ship Made
> Famous in the Novels of Patrick O'Brian*** (US subtitle) / ***…The Design, Construction and
> Careers of Jack Aubrey's Favourite Command*** (UK subtitle).
> Conway (Anova), London, 8 November 2008, ISBN 978-1-84486-074-6 / 1-84486-074-4.
> W. W. Norton, New York, 2009, ISBN 978-0-393-07009-5 / 0-393-07009-3 (also cited as
> 9780393070620). 144 pp., 31 cm.
> Sources: https://archive.org/details/frigatesurprisec0000lave (IA metadata: ISBN
> 9780393070095, "144 p. : 31 cm", "First published in 2008 by Conway"); LCCN 2008039603;
> OCLC 1149173725; OpenLibrary OL22528792M.

Marquardt is illustrator/draughtsman, not author. He was born Hamburg 9 Nov 1927, died 2019,
emigrated to Australia 1966, Honorary Curator for Ship Models, Melbourne Maritime Museum
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Heinz_Marquardt).

**Consequence for a modeller:** there is no 1:64 / 1:48 AOTS-style large-format plan sheet
set to buy. There is (a) an 8-page drawing chapter in a 144-page trade book, and (b) the
original Admiralty draughts of Surprise herself at the NMM, which are purchasable. See §6.

## 1. The book's plan content (what Marquardt actually drew)

Verbatim contents list, from the Ships of Scale book review by "Uwek", 7 May 2018
(https://shipsofscale.com/sosforums/threads/book-review-the-frigate-surprise-by-brian-lavery-geoff-hunt.1961/):

```
Introduction by Nikolai Tolstoy
1) L'Unité Joins the Royal Navy            p.  8–19
2) The Voyage to Trieste                   p. 20–33
3) Captain Hamilton                        p. 34–45
4) A visit on Board                        p. 46–63
5) The Drawings by Karl Heinz Marquardt    p. 64–71
6) The West Indies                         p. 72–83
7) Taking the Hermione                     p. 84–93
8) The Last Years                          p. 94–103
9) Jack Aubrey's Surprise                  p. 104–115
10) Constructing a Picture of Surprise by Geoff Hunt  p. 116–123
The Aubrey/Maturin Novels cover by cover   p. 124–135
Appendices: Crew list; Masts, Yards and Stores; Maintenance History; Armaments
Endnotes, Bibliography, original sources, Glossary, Index
```

- **Marquardt's dedicated drawing chapter = 8 pages (pp. 64–71).** Further Marquardt line
  work is scattered through the text ("partly in sheets, partly inside the text" — same
  review).
- **Number of drawings: sources disagree.**
  - "more than 50 specially commissioned line plans by draughtsman Karl Heinz Marquardt" —
    publisher blurb, quoted at https://www.abebooks.com/9781844860746/Frigate-Surprise-Design-Construction-Careers-1844860744/plp
  - "Fifty line and isometric plans" — same family of blurbs.
  - "some 30 line drawings" — the Conway synopsis reproduced verbatim in the Ships of Scale
    review (link above).
  - **Trust ~50.** The "30" figure sits in a sentence that also says "some 30 paintings by
    co-author Geoff Hunt", and the two counts appear to have been transposed in that blurb;
    every independent retailer listing says 50+.
- Drawing types stated: **line plans and isometric (cutaway/axonometric) plans**
  (https://www.historicnavalfiction.com/other-genres/aos-naval-non-fiction/famous-ships/frigatesurprise).
- **Two sail plans are drawn:** one "per historical records" and one "as described in the
  O'Brian novels, with the mainmast of a 36-gun frigate" (retailer/blurb text surfaced for
  https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9781844860746/Frigate-Surprise-Brian-Lavery-1844860744/plp).
  This is the single most important fact in the book for a modeller: **Marquardt drew both
  the real rig and the fictional tall rig, and they are different ships aloft.**

### Sheer / body / half-breadth content — NOT FOUND at itemised level
No source I could reach lists the individual sheets (i.e. "sheer plan, body plan, half-breadth,
inboard profile, deck plans, midship section, framing…"). The book is not in Google Books
preview (Google Books API returns no volume for the title+author), and the Internet Archive
copy is lending-restricted with search-inside disabled (verified: `/fulltext/inside.php` and
`api.archivelab.org` both return errors for item `frigatesurprisec0000lave`).

**RECONSTRUCTED sheet list** — Marquardt's house style in his three AOTS volumes is
consistent, so expect: general arrangement, lines (sheer/body/half-breadth), inboard profile,
deck plans (upper deck, quarterdeck & forecastle, platforms/hold), midship section,
stem/stern/framing details, external decoration elevations, masting & rigging elevation,
sail plan, ordnance and boats. Mark as inference, not fact.

### Plan scale in the book
- One reader measured the printed scale bar: **"157 mm = 100 feet"** — pugwash, post #8,
  9 June 2024, same Ships of Scale thread.
- He guessed "1:157?". **That is wrong arithmetic.** 100 ft = 30 480 mm, so
  30 480 / 157 = **1:194.1**. At that scale a 126 ft hull prints 198 mm long — consistent with
  a 310 mm-wide page.
- Treat as ONE unverified measurement off one page. **Do not scale geometry from the book
  pages.** Use the NMM draughts (§6).

## 2. Principal dimensions

All Royal Navy figures are as measured/recorded after capture. Wikipedia's numbers and
threedecks' both derive from Rif Winfield, *British Warships in the Age of Sail 1793–1817*
(threedecks source code `BWAS-1793`).

| Dimension | Imperial | Metric (m) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length on gundeck | 126 ft 0 in | 38.405 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Surprise_(1796) ; https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=6983 |
| Length of keel (for tonnage) | 108 ft 6⅛ in | 33.074 | Wikipedia (as above) |
| Length of keel — variant | 108 ft 6¼ in | 33.077 | threedecks (as above) |
| Breadth (extreme) | 31 ft 8 in | 9.652 | both, agree |
| Depth in hold | 10 ft 0 in | 3.048 | Wikipedia |
| Depth in hold — variant | 10 ft 0½ in | 3.061 | threedecks |
| Draught | 14 ft 0½ in | 4.280 | Wikipedia |
| Burthen | 578 73/94 tons bm | — | both, agree |
| Displacement | 657 tons | — | Wikipedia; repeated by Vanguard Models https://vanguardmodels.co.uk/products/hms-surprise |

**Arithmetic check (mine).** Builder's Old Measurement is
`(keel_for_tonnage × beam × beam/2) / 94`. Using 108 ft 6⅛ in and 31 ft 8 in:
(108.5104 × 31.6667 × 15.8333) / 94 = **578.78 tons**, against the recorded **578 73/94 =
578.777**. Exact. Using 108 ft 6¼ in gives 578.83 — slightly worse.
→ **Use 108 ft 6⅛ in = 33.074 m.** The 6¼ in variant is a transcription slip.

**WARNING — threedecks metric column is buggy.** It converts the keel as 32.9248 m, which is
108 ft 0¼ in, not 108 ft 6¼ in: their parser dropped the feet/inches boundary. Its breadth
(9.652) and depth (3.0607) conversions are correct. Do not trust threedecks' metric column
without re-deriving it. (Same bug on the sister page for HMS *Unite*, id=7261: 142 ft 5½ in
is given as 43.2943 m; correct value is 43.421 m.)

### Competing "129 ft" figure — do not use
A 2012 user comment on the same threedecks page (Ian Johnston, 16 May 2012) and the Military
Wiki mirror give: Tons burthen 350 bm, **Length 129 ft (39 m)**, Beam 31 ft 8 in, Draught 4.2 m,
Complement 240. Source: https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/HMS_Surprise_(1796) ,
threedecks comment thread.
**These are the FRENCH figures for the corvette Unité** (350 *tonneaux* burthen is confirmed
by Winfield & Roberts, *French Warships in the Age of Sail 1786–1861*, cited on
https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=6984). 129 ft is almost certainly
a French-measure length converted loosely. **For geometry use the British 126 ft 0 in
gundeck**, because that is the figure that reconciles with the recorded tonnage (above) and
with the Admiralty draught taken off at Plymouth.

### Derived proportions (my arithmetic, for hull fairing)
- Length:beam on gundeck = 126.000 / 31.667 = **3.979 : 1**
- Beam:depth in hold = 31.667 / 10.042 = **3.154 : 1**
- Gundeck:keel = 126.000 / 108.510 = **1.161 : 1** (→ combined stem rake + sternpost rake
  accounts for 17 ft 5⅞ in / 5.331 m of overhang along the deck line)
- She is small. Contemporary frigates were "over 150 feet long and reckoned at 1000 tons or
  more" (http://wiki.hmssurprise.org/phase3/index.php/HMS_Surprise_(ship)).

## 3. Spar dimensions

The book has an appendix "**Masts, Yards and Stores**" (contents list, §1). I could not read
it. But the two load-bearing numbers Lavery published are quoted, with his book cited as the
authority, in WikiPOBia (archived copy, since the live site is down:
http://web.archive.org/web/20230930162819/http://wiki.hmssurprise.org/phase3/index.php/HMS_Surprise_(ship) ,
footnotes 1 and 2).

### DOCUMENTED — Surprise's own spars, from the 1802 sale inventory (via Lavery)

| Spar | Imperial | Metric (m) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower mainmast | 71 ft 0 in | 21.641 | Lavery & Hunt 2008, cited WikiPOBia fn.2 (archived URL above) |
| Lower foremast | 66 ft 0 in | 20.117 | same |

Lavery's finding, verbatim in substance from the same page: "the record … of the ship's
fittings at the time of sale in 1802 seems consistent with this, **all the spars being
appropriate to a 24- or even 20-gun ship**."

Supporting document: **a letter from Captain Hamilton, May 1798, instructs the dockyard to
remove the mainmast and set up the existing foremast in its place**, to make the ship stiffer
(same source). So the tall rig, if it existed at all, was a short-lived 1796–98 experiment.

### CONTEXT — Burney's rated table (the "what she should have had" figures)

William Burney, *A New Universal Dictionary of the Marine* (1815), article 'Mast', table of
lower mainmast lengths, quoted at WikiPOBia fn.2 (archived URL above):

| Rating | Lower mainmast | Metric (m) |
|---|---|---|
| 36-gun ship | 89 ft 0 in | 27.127 |
| 28-gun ship | 81 ft 0 in | 24.689 |
| 20-gun ship | 71 ft 0 in | 21.641 |

Note that Surprise's actual 1802 mainmast (71 ft) is **exactly Burney's 20-gun figure**.

### The rig Geoff Hunt paints (and which Marquardt's second sail plan represents)

Geoff Hunt, *The Marine Art of Geoff Hunt* (Conway Maritime, 2004), p. 114, cited at WikiPOBia
fn.2: Hunt **adopts the 89 ft (36-gun) mainmast**, and reckons the **overall height of the
main-topgallant truck as 146 ft 0 in = 44.501 m** above (presumably) the waterline.

### RECONSTRUCTED spar set — three rig options

I could find no published full spar table. Below I reconstruct three internally consistent
sets. **Rule used, and its source:** the only two Surprise-specific spar lengths that survive
(main 71 ft, fore 66 ft, 1802 inventory via Lavery) give the ship's own
**fore:main ratio = 66/71 = 0.9296**; Hunt's pairing of an 89 ft mainmast with a 146 ft truck
height gives **truck:mainmast = 146/89 = 1.6404**. I scale both ratios across the three
mainmast options. This is arithmetic on sourced pairs, not a period rule — treat accordingly.

| | A. As-fitted 1802 (documented main) | B. Burney 28-gun (rated) | C. Fictional / O'Brian 36-gun |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower mainmast | **71 ft 0 in / 21.641 m** (documented) | 81 ft 0 in / 24.689 m (Burney) | 89 ft 0 in / 27.127 m (Burney; adopted by Hunt) |
| Lower foremast | **66 ft 0 in / 20.117 m** (documented) | ~75 ft 4 in / 22.95 m (RECONSTRUCTED, ×0.9296) | ~82 ft 9 in / 25.22 m (RECONSTRUCTED, ×0.9296) |
| Main truck above waterline | ~116 ft 6 in / 35.51 m (RECONSTRUCTED, ×1.6404) | ~132 ft 11 in / 40.50 m (RECONSTRUCTED, ×1.6404) | **146 ft 0 in / 44.501 m** (Hunt, documented) |

Mainmast : extreme beam ratios, for sanity-checking a model:
A = 71/31.667 = 2.242; B = 2.558; C = 2.811.

**Lower mizzen, all topmasts, topgallants, bowsprit, jib-boom, all yards, all spar diameters:
NOT FOUND.** No accessible source quotes them for this ship. Do not invent them from the
Rose/replica — see §5.

## 4. Armament — four competing records

| # | Date / status | Upper (main) deck | Quarterdeck | Forecastle | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | As French **Unité**, 1794 | 24 × 8-pdr long | 8 × 4-pdr long | — | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Surprise_(1796) |
| 2 | As French **Unité**, 16 Jan 1794 | 22 × French 8-pdr | 2 × French 12-pdr (QD/Fc combined) | — | Winfield & Roberts, *French Warships in the Age of Sail 1786–1861*, via https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=6984 . Broadside 100 French livres = 107.92 lb = 48.95 kg |
| 3 | **Recommended** by Plymouth Dockyard, 1796/98 (never fitted) | 24 × 9-pdr long | 8 × 4-pdr long + 4 × 12-pdr carronade | 2 × 4-pdr long + 2 × 12-pdr carronade | Winfield BWAS-1793 via https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=6983 . Broadside 164 lb / 74.374 kg |
| 4 | **Actually received on board, 21 April 1798** | **24 × 32-pdr carronade** | **8 × 18-pdr carronade** | **2 × 18-pdr carronade + 2 × 4-pdr long** | Richard Wright, comment of 2 April 2026 on the threedecks Surprise page (link above), stating "Specific evidence uncovered from research with original documentation from correspondence between Navy Board, Captain Hamilton and Dockyard during refit after capture" |

Also on record: 24 × 32-pdr carronade + 10 × 32-pdr carronade + 2 × 4-pdr chasers
(Ian Johnston, 2012 comment, same page — a variant of #4).

**Which to model.** Record #4 is the only one claiming primary dockyard correspondence and it
explicitly contradicts #3 as "recommended … never enacted". A carronade-armed Surprise is
also what the Vanguard kit offers as its *historical* option, against the 9-pdr long guns as
its *fictional* option (https://vanguardmodels.co.uk/products/hms-surprise). **Model #4 for
the real 1798–1802 ship; #3 (24 × 9-pdr) for the O'Brian ship.** Flag #4 as a single
researcher's forum claim, unpublished.

Fremantle's capture despatch (Inconstant, 23 April 1796, *London Gazette* 28 May 1796,
reproduced at https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=6984) calls her
"**L'Unite, a Corvette of 34 gun and 218 men**" — a fifth count, and a reminder that nominal
gun numbers for this ship are worthless.

Complement: 200 (June 1796, Winfield, threedecks); 172 peace / 220 war (Wikipedia);
240 (Johnston comment); 218 at capture (Fremantle, *London Gazette*).
Establishment "between 200 and 240" (WikiPOBia archived).

Rating history: French corvette → RN **28-gun Sixth Rate frigate** (Winfield/threedecks);
"she briefly enjoyed the status of a fifth rate ship before reverting to the sixth rate"
(WikiPOBia archived). Ship-rigged throughout.

## 5. Decoration and figurehead

**NOT FOUND for the real ship.** No description of Surprise's figurehead, stern board, or
quarter-gallery carving is quoted in any accessible source, and the NMM catalogue entry for
her own draught (ZAZ3067, §6) does not mention a figurehead in its description — unlike the
NMM entry for the *other* Unite, which explicitly records "sternboard decoration and name in
a cartouche on stern counter … and figurehead".

Two negative findings a modeller must act on:

1. **The famous female warrior with sword and shield is not documented.** It appears to be
   Geoff Hunt's invention for the book jackets, subsequently carved onto the film replica.
   Surfaced via search over https://www.hmssurprise.org/hms-surprise-cover and
   https://jamesjchoi.com/sculptures/7-sculptures-surprise-figurehead-and-ornaments ; I could
   not open a page that states it in those words, so treat as **low confidence**.
2. **Kit carvings copy the replica, not the ship.** Of the ZHL/ShiCheng 1:48 kit: "The kit
   seems to be a cross between the plans of the original Unité by John Marshall, and the
   replica HMS Surprise (Rose). **The figurehead and stern carvings are exactly from the
   replica ship.**" — Jweb, 30 Oct 2021,
   https://shipsofscale.com/sosforums/threads/hms-surprise-1-48-from-zhl.8480/

The same post records replica-derived errors to avoid: "the forward gunport is missing for the
main battery and on the forecastle. The position of the hawseholes are too low — again similar
to the replica ship."

**The film ship is not this ship.** Vanguard Models: "The film version is much smaller than the
real (and fictional) Surprise, as this was based on a 20-gun Seaford Class ship from the
1750's, with a gun deck length **around 20 foot shorter** than the real Surprise with less main
deck gun port piercings and substantially fewer upper deck guns."
(https://vanguardmodels.co.uk/products/hms-surprise). 20 ft = 6.10 m, i.e. the film hull is
~106 ft / 32.3 m on the gundeck.

## 6. Marquardt's source material — and the "sister ship" question

**The brief's premise that Marquardt reconstructed her from a sister/similar vessel's draught
appears to be FALSE, and rests on a confusion between two different French ships both called
Unité, both captured within a week in April 1796.**

### The two ships

| | **L'Unité (1794)** → HMS **Surprise** | **Unité** (ex-*La Variante* 1787) → HMS **Unite** |
|---|---|---|
| threedecks id | 6984 → 6983 | 7261 |
| Type | Unité-class corvette, 6th rate | Charmante-class frigate, 5th rate |
| Designer / yard | Pierre-Alexandre Forfait; built Jean Fouache, Le Havre | Jacques Denis Chevillard le Cadet; Rochefort |
| Launched | 16 Jan 1794 | 1787 |
| Captured | **20 Apr 1796** by *Inconstant* | **12 Apr 1796** |
| Gundeck | 126 ft 0 in / 38.405 m | 142 ft 5½ in / 43.421 m |
| Keel | 108 ft 6⅛ in / 33.074 m | 118 ft 5⅛ in / 36.098 m |
| Breadth | 31 ft 8 in / 9.652 m | 37 ft 8 in / 11.481 m |
| Depth in hold | 10 ft 0½ in / 3.061 m | 11 ft 0 in / 3.353 m |
| Burthen | 578 73/94 bm | 873 71/94 bm |

Renaming reason, confirmed: she "was renamed (since the Royal Navy already possessed a ship
called Unité, taken just a week previously)" — WikiPOBia (archived URL, §3).

### The NMM draughts — three plans, two different ships

| NMM ref | Titled | Which ship | Content | Date | Scale | Sheet size | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ZAZ3067** | "Lines & Profile" | **SURPRISE (captured 1796)** | lines and profile | 1798 | not stated in record | 470 × 1350 mm | https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-82858 |
| **ZAZ3068** | "Deck, Quarter & Forecastle" | **SURPRISE (captured 1796)** | deck, quarterdeck & forecastle plans; sheet inscribed "SURPRISE 1796" | 1798 | not stated | 280 × 935 mm | https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-82859 |
| **ZAZ3181** | "Unite (1796)" | the **OTHER** ship (ex-*Variante*) | body plan with sternboard decoration and name in cartouche on stern counter, sheer lines with inboard detail and figurehead, longitudinal half-breadth | July 1796 | **1:48** | 463 × 1342 mm | https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-82972 |

ZAZ3181's own description says it is "as taken off at Plymouth Dockyard prior to fitting as a
**32-gun, Fifth Rate Frigate**" — a 5th-rate 32, which matches HMS *Unite* (26 × 12-pdr on the
upper gundeck, October 1796, per threedecks id 7261) and cannot be the 28-gun 6th-rate
Surprise. **If a secondary source ever says "Marquardt used the draught of Unite", that is
ZAZ3181 and it is the wrong ship.** I found no source that actually says this.

All three plans are **signed J. / John Marshall, Master Shipwright, Plymouth Dockyard
1795–1801**. The ZAZ3067 record carries the Progress Book provenance verbatim: "The progress
book, volume five, folio 268, states that 'Surprize' arrived at Plymouth dockyard on 21
January 1798, was docked on 15 February to be re-coppered and launched on 2 March. She sailed
having been fitted on 2 May 1798."
(https://images.rmg.co.uk/asset/16481/ — asset caption surfaced in search; RMG blocks direct
fetch. Same text at https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-82858 .)

Print of ZAZ3067 available: "Plan of HMS 'Surprise', 1796, lines and profile", artist John
Marshall, image 1350 × 470 mm, NMM image ref **J5948** —
https://prints.rmg.co.uk/products/plan-of-hms-surprise-1796-lines-and-profile-j5948

### What secondary sources actually say about Marquardt's source

- Modellers treat the Marquardt plans and the Marshall draughts as the same lineage:
  "after all, the **draft plans by Marshall are available for purchase at the NMM**" — Jweb,
  https://shipsofscale.com/sosforums/threads/hms-surprise-1-48-from-zhl.8480/
- **Marquardt's plans are dimensionally checkable and were checked:** "The dimensions of the
  hull and the positioning of the gunports, hatches and masts **correspond exactly to plans by
  KH Marquardt** once they are scaled to **1:46**. This is the true scale of this kit."
  — same post. (This is the kit's true scale, not the plan's; the point is that Marquardt's
  geometry is internally coherent and reproducible.)
- **Marquardt's published views are not perfectly consistent with one another.** A 3D modeller
  working from high-res scans of the book's plans: "This is when I realised **the draught
  plans were all slightly out in each view**. … the overall consensus was these plans are never
  100% accurate and it's going to involve me using my initiative to overcome this."
  — Beck, 17 Jan 2020, Model Ship World topic 22892, archived copy
  http://web.archive.org/web/20250519221129/https://modelshipworld.com/topic/22892-hms-surprise-1796-formally-unit%C3%A9-my-first-ship-model/
  This is the closest thing to a documented reliability criticism I could find. It concerns
  cross-view registration (bow especially), not the hull's overall correctness.
- Another modeller working from the book confirms the drawings are the working source for 3D
  work: "doing my best to stick the drawings and blue prints provided in 'The Frigate Surprise'
  by Brian Lavery & Geof hunt" — CarnyRex, 21 Feb 2024,
  https://shipsofscale.com/sosforums/threads/understanding-bow-details-hms-surprise-lunite-1-48.13786/

### Marquardt's own caveats — NOT FOUND
His chapter text (pp. 64–71) is not reproduced anywhere I could reach. No direct quotation of
any Marquardt statement about what he conjectured survives online. **The one structural
conjecture that is certain from the book's own description is the rig:** he drew two sail
plans, one historical and one fictional (§1), which means at least the fictional one is openly
declared conjectural. Lavery's own text undermines the tall-mast tradition (§3), so the
"36-gun mainmast" sail plan should be treated as an illustration of O'Brian's ship, not of the
1796 ship.

## 9. Recommended action for the modeller

1. **Buy NMM ZAZ3067 (lines & profile) and ZAZ3068 (deck/QD/Fc) reproductions.** These are the
   only true primary geometry for this hull, they are of Surprise herself, and they are for
   sale. Print ref J5948 for ZAZ3067.
2. Use 126 ft 0 in gundeck / 108 ft 6⅛ in keel / 31 ft 8 in beam / 10 ft 0½ in depth in hold.
   These reconcile exactly with the 578 73/94 tons bm record.
3. Buy Lavery & Hunt 2008 (ISBN 9781844860746 / 9780393070095) for the Marquardt chapter and
   the "Masts, Yards and Stores" appendix. Nothing online substitutes for it.
4. Decide **rig option A, B or C (§3)** before modelling anything above the deck. A and C
   differ by 18 ft (5.49 m) of lower mainmast and ~30 ft (9 m) of truck height.
5. Do not copy the replica *Rose* / film ship for the figurehead, stern carving, hawse-hole
   position or forward gunports (§5).
