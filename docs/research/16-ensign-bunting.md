# Naval ensign and working bunting — 4 October 2026

The library retains a blue ensign with a pre-1801 Union by default, matching its
1798 modelling datum. A host may select `ensign: 'white' | 'blue' | 'red'` and
`flagYear: 1805`. The Helmsman's Watch scene selects the white ensign and the
post-1801 Union to give its main flag a clearly visible naval design. This is
an artistic squadron-colour choice, not a claim that Surprise always wore white.
The white pattern includes a red St George cross across the entire field;
simply recolouring the old blue field would not produce that design.

Evidence used:

- [RMG AAA0937, White Ensign, circa 1794](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-937):
  surviving hand-sewn wool bunting, associated with Brunswick. The catalogue
  distinguishes possible squadron colours from colours carried in an action.
- [RMG ZBA6946, Minotaur Union flag](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-618890):
  post-1801 pattern, hand-sewn wool bunting, early nineteenth century and
  believed flown at Trafalgar. The saltire's asymmetric construction is visible
  in the object description; its later replacement hoist is not used as evidence
  for the original flag's fastening.

The delivered flag is an original procedural drawing. No museum photograph is
copied. Raised breadth seams, double hem stitches, a pale reinforced heading,
subtle weave, modest panel-to-panel dye variation and fly fading suggest sewn
working cloth. The exact seam layout, stitch sizes and wear are reconstructed
visual details, not measured features of Surprise's lost ensign. The existing
flag dimensions are retained. No battle damage or large artificial tears are
added.

Colour, bump and roughness maps use the same UVs and move with the existing
wind-driven mesh. A small directional backlighting term suggests light passing
through wool without transparent surface sorting. The texture and shader cost
is limited to the flags, with no added geometry or draw calls. The reverse is
the reverse of one piece of cloth; the canton is not duplicated at both ends.
